# Turkic interlanguage observation notebook

Status: append-only research scaffold; **not** an interlanguage, bridge lexicon,
grammar, proof grammar, reader, or automated realization system.

## Controlling constraints

- Turkish Open Logic translation remains the production task through OLP-0722.
- Turkish is one observed language surface, never the default Turkic pivot or a
  proxy for Azerbaijani, Kazakh, Kyrgyz, Uzbek, Uyghur, or another standard.
- Record named-language evidence and family/cohort dependence separately.
- Preserve script, morphology, syntax, register, and conceptual differences;
  similarity is a hypothesis to test, not permission to merge.
- A candidate bridge item remains a candidate until it has source provenance,
  at least two independently evidenced language surfaces, adverse evidence,
  reversible mappings, and explicit cohort limits.
- Do not promote observations into accepted terms or grammar rules from Turkish
  intuition alone. No scalar score may substitute for typed evidence.

## Seed observations from Turkish production

### TIN-0001 — terminology is not a context-free word list

Open Logic repeatedly distinguishes codomain, range, function value, and image.
The Turkish reader therefore uses context-bound surfaces (`değer kümesi`,
`görüntü kümesi`, `fonksiyon değeri`, and a subset's `görüntüsü`) rather than
collapsing these into one apparently cognate item. Any future pivot must encode
the typed concept and role first; lexical resemblance across Turkic languages is
insufficient. Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0002 — productive suffixes belong to language realizers

The Open Logic token system exposed that a shared bare term cannot safely carry
literal Turkish case, number, or possessive suffixes. Vowel harmony and consonant
alternation required Turkish-specific phrasing or inflection. A future abstract
layer may carry grammatical features, but each named language must realize them
independently; Turkish suffix strings must never be exported as pan-Turkic
morphology. Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0003 — internationalisms still need adverse mappings

Terms such as `fonksiyon`, `tableau`, `sekant`, and `semantik` can look like easy
cross-language anchors, but their acceptance, spelling, inflection, and reader
register vary. Record them as search crosswalks with named-language evidence and
competitors, not as automatically preferred bridge vocabulary. Evidence state:
observation.

### TIN-0004 — conceptual collisions must survive the pivot

Turkish production keeps mathematical `ayrık` (disjoint) separate from the
components of a disjunction, `ispat` separate from formal `türetim`, and
`Dedekind kesimi` separate from order-theoretic `başlangıç kesiti`. A future
pivot must represent these distinctions as separate typed nodes even where one
language happens to reuse a surface. Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0005 — script and cohort are first-class evidence

The current lane corpus includes Latin, Cyrillic, and Arabic-script surfaces.
Transliteration can support search and comparison but cannot be treated as the
canonical form or as evidence of lexical identity. Every observation must retain
the original script, named standard, source, and cohort edge. Evidence state:
observation.

### TIN-0006 — metalogical relation families need typed separation

The axiomatic-deduction material distinguishes semantic consequence, formal
derivability, theoremhood, consistency, satisfiability, soundness, and
completeness. Turkish realizes these with related but non-interchangeable
surfaces (`mantıksal gerektirme`, `türetilebilirlik`, `teorem olma`,
`tutarlılık`, `sağlanabilirlik`, `sağlamlık`, `tamlık`). A future pivot must
encode the relation, level, and direction; it must not infer equivalence from
shared suffixes or ordinary-language proximity. Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0007 — loans can be stable labels without being bridge defaults

`Modus ponens` is retained in the Turkish reader, but its inferential pattern is
also stated explicitly. For interlanguage work, a retained international label
can be a crosswalk key while the typed rule supplies meaning. Whether another
named language retains, transliterates, or replaces that label remains an
independent realization decision. Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0008 — formal signs must not be flattened into lexical negation

The tableau chapter uses `işaretli formül` for a formula paired with a truth
sign. The sign is metalinguistic and is not the same object as formula-internal
negation. A future abstract layer must type sign, signed formula, truth value,
and negation separately even if a named language uses visually or lexically
similar expressions. Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0009 — closure requires an explicit object and domain

`Kapalı` occurs in several unrelated mathematical constructions. In the
tableau material, closure is a property of a branch or tableau produced by a
contradictory signed pair; it must not merge with algebraic closure,
topological closure, closed terms, or function-closed sets. Candidate nodes
must carry both the operation/property and the object type. Evidence state:
observation.

### TIN-0010 — identity, equality notation, and equivalence are distinct

Turkish prose can use nearby surfaces for identity, equality, and semantic or
syntactic equivalence. The translation keeps the identity predicate, equality
symbol, equivalence relation, and equivalence class as separate typed objects.
Any future bridge representation must preserve those levels and cannot infer a
merge from a shared stem. Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0011 — notation may anchor comparison but cannot choose the language

Tableau rule symbols and proof-tree geometry provide useful cross-language
alignment points because their formal roles are stable under translation. They
can locate corresponding passages and typed rules, but they do not license a
shared lexical surface or Turkish-derived morphology. Evidence state:
observation.

### TIN-0012 — derivational word families still require typed members

The Natural Deduction material uses distinct concepts for derive, derivation,
derivable, derivability, and nonderivability. Turkish realizes them through a
productive `türet-` family, but the shared stem does not make the logical roles
interchangeable. A future abstract layer should link the family while retaining
each predicate, event, artifact, and metaproperty as its own node. Evidence
state: observation.

### TIN-0013 — discharge is an operation on labelled occurrences

Natural-deduction discharge closes selected, labelled assumption occurrences;
it is not ordinary deletion or a language-independent “emptying” metaphor.
Any candidate representation should encode the assumption occurrence, label,
subderivation scope, and rule that licenses closure before a named language
chooses its verb. Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0014 — eigenvariable status is formal, not lexical

An eigenvariable condition is fundamentally a freshness and dependency
constraint, while the displayed symbol may formally belong to the source
system's constant vocabulary. A cross-language record should type the formal
role and freshness scope separately from the local term used to explain the
symbol category. Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0015 — structural-rule words need calculus-scoped concepts

Sequent-calculus terms such as contraction, exchange, weakening, antecedent,
and consequent use ordinary-looking mathematical or grammatical vocabulary.
Their abstract nodes must be scoped to a rule schema, side of the sequent, and
sequence transformation; a lexical match must not merge contraction with
function restriction or exchange with ordinary permutation. Evidence state:
observation.

### TIN-0016 — register competitors are adverse crosswalks, not duplicates

The Turkish edition selects `tıkızlık` for logical compactness while
`kompaktlık` remains a discoverability competitor. A future interlanguage
record should retain both with source, register, and domain evidence, but map
them to one typed logical concept only within justified cohorts. It must not
select either surface for another Turkic language by default. Evidence state:
observation.

### TIN-0017 — formula, sentence, and proposition require object typing

The completeness and model-theory chapters depend on the distinction between a
formula, a closed formula/sentence, and a proposition expressed in prose.
Turkish uses `formül`, `tümce`, and context-sensitive `önerme`, but those
surfaces are not a ready-made regional bridge. A future abstract layer must
record free-variable status and object role before attaching any named-language
realization. Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0018 — completeness is a family of domain-scoped concepts

`Tamlık` occurs as a metatheorem relating derivability to semantic consequence,
as completeness of a theory or consistent set, and as an order/field property.
Shared morphology must not merge these. Candidate records need the bearer,
quantification, and defining relation, with local register competitors retained
as evidence rather than promoted across Turkic standards. Evidence state:
observation.

### TIN-0019 — structural sameness has graded, directional relations

Model theory distinguishes reduct, expansion, substructure, extension,
isomorphism, partial isomorphism, and elementary equivalence. Some are directed
inclusion/forgetful relations; others are symmetric equivalences of different
strength. A bridge graph must encode direction, preserved signature, domain
map, and logical strength instead of aligning these by the broad Turkish words
`yapı`, `genişleme`, or `eşdeğerlik`. Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0020 — representation, coding, and interpretation need separate edges

The incompleteness material uses a formula to represent a numerical function
or relation inside a theory, while separately coding syntax by numbers and
interpreting non-logical symbols in a structure. Turkish can place all three
near ordinary `temsil` language, but their witnesses and directions differ. A
future bridge graph must type formula-to-function representation,
syntax-to-number coding, and symbol-to-denotation interpretation separately.
This Turkish observation supplies no default surface for another Turkic
standard. Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0021 — machine outcomes and decision strength need typed separation

The Turing-machine material distinguishes a run's halting or divergence, an
accept/reject convention, total decision, partial recognition or
semidecision, and effective enumeration. Turkish uses related operational
phrasing (`durmak`, `kabul/ret`, `karar verilebilir`, `yarı karar
verilebilir`, `numaralandırılabilir`), but neither shared stems nor a chosen
halting-state convention makes these concepts interchangeable. A future
bridge graph must type the input/output condition, totality requirement, and
machine convention before adding any named-language surface; this Turkish
observation supplies no default realization for another Turkic standard.
Evidence state: observation.

### TIN-0022 — refutability transforms are not set complements

The theories-and-computability material defines $\Th{\bar Q}$ as the
sentences whose negations are provable in $\Th{Q}$, while
$\Complement{C}$ is an actual set-theoretic complement and a computable
separator is a third object constrained by two inclusions. In an incomplete
theory, the refutability transform need not be the complement of the
theorem set. A future bridge graph must therefore type negation,
refutability, complement, separator, and many-one reduction as distinct
operations or relations; bar notation or similar local vocabulary must not
trigger an automatic merge. No Turkish surface is proposed as a default for
another Turkic standard. Evidence state: observation.
